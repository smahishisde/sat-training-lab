Targeted Lesson 4 — Counting Solutions & Intersections

WHEN I SEE 'how many solutions' with an absolute value or parameter → think intersections before solving individual equations.

Take your time here. The goal is to understand the pattern well enough that a different-looking SAT question still feels familiar.

See the structure first

Think visually: the number of x-solutions equals the number of intersections.

Worked example — teacher walkthrough

Translate algebra into a picture

Take |x²-9|=k. Instead of immediately splitting absolute-value cases, think of two graphs: y=|x²-9| and y=k.

What does y=k look like?

It is just a horizontal line. Changing k moves that line up or down.

What does y=|x²-9| look like?

Start with y=x²-9, a parabola shifted down 9. The parts below the x-axis get flipped upward by the absolute value. That creates a W-like shape.

Now count instead of solve

Every place the horizontal line y=k touches the W-shaped graph gives one x-value, so one real solution.

What usually happens

For a positive k less than 9, the line cuts the W shape four times. Four intersections = four solutions.

What happens at the special height k=9

The two inner intersections collapse into the single point x=0. The two outer intersections remain. Total: three distinct solutions.

Why Desmos is excellent here

You can graph y=abs(x²-9) and y=k. Move k and literally watch the number of intersections change. This is faster and safer than grinding four algebra cases when the question only asks for the number of solutions.

What makes this hard on the SAT

The problem looks like an equation-solving problem. The real task is recognizing that the parameter controls a graph and that the SAT is asking you to count intersections.

What to write first on test day: Write/think: # solutions = # intersections

Common wrong turn

Do not count branches; count distinct x-values. Two algebraic branches can meet at the same x.

Guided example

For what positive k does |x²-25|=k have exactly 3 distinct real solutions?

The special height equals the depth of the original parabola below the x-axis: 25.


5-question transfer check

Do not rush into these. First ask yourself: “What pattern from the lesson is hiding here?”
