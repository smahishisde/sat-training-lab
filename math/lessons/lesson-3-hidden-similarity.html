Targeted Lesson 3

Hidden Similarity in Right Triangles

WHEN I SEE an altitude from the right angle to the hypotenuse → expect three similar triangles.

Let’s learn this like a tutor would teach it

This is one of those SAT geometry patterns that feels random until you see the picture clearly.

Imagine a right triangle. Now draw an altitude from the 90° corner straight down to the hypotenuse.

That one line creates two smaller right triangles inside the original. The surprising part is that all three triangles are similar to each other.

Worked example — talk it through with me

Label the two pieces of the hypotenuse

Call them p and q. Then the full hypotenuse is c=p+q.

Why similarity appears

Each small triangle has a right angle, and each shares one acute angle with the original triangle. Two matching angles are enough for AA similarity.

The altitude shortcut

If h is the altitude, then h²=pq. Think of h as the geometric mean of the two hypotenuse pieces.

Try actual numbers

Suppose p=4 and q=9. Then h²=4×9=36, so h=6.

Do not stop too early

The SAT may not ask for h. It may ask for area, a leg, or some other quantity.

If it asks for area

The full hypotenuse is 4+9=13. Use the hypotenuse as the base and the altitude as the height: area = 1/2×13×6 = 39.

The leg shortcuts

If c=p+q, then the leg next to p satisfies leg²=cp, and the leg next to q satisfies other leg²=cq.

What I want you to remember: Altitude to the hypotenuse → three similar triangles. First write c=p+q and h²=pq.

Common wrong turn

A very common mistake is finding the altitude correctly and then answering with the altitude even when the question asks for area or a leg.

Try one with me

Guided check: The altitude divides the hypotenuse into 9 and 16. What is the altitude?

If this still feels unclear, go back to the worked example before continuing.

5-question transfer check

Now prove you can recognize the same idea when the SAT changes the presentation.

Near transfer
1. The altitude to a right triangle's hypotenuse divides it into segments 4 and 9. Find the altitude.
Different presentation
2. The altitude divides the hypotenuse into 5 and 20. Find the area of the original triangle.
SAT-style trap
3. A right triangle has hypotenuse 25. One hypotenuse segment adjacent to a leg is 9. Find that leg.
Strategy choice
4. The altitude divides a hypotenuse into 8 and 18. What is the altitude?
Far transfer
5. A right triangle's altitude to its hypotenuse divides the hypotenuse into 9 and 16. Find the shorter leg.
