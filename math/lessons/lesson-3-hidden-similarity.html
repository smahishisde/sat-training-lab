Targeted Lesson 3 — Hidden Similarity in Right Triangles

WHEN I SEE an altitude from the right angle to the hypotenuse → expect three similar triangles.

Take your time here. The goal is to understand the pattern well enough that a different-looking SAT question still feels familiar.

See the structure first

The dashed altitude creates two smaller right triangles. All three triangles are similar.

Worked example — teacher walkthrough

Start with the picture, not the formula

This pattern only makes sense if you see the geometry. We have one big right triangle. Then an altitude is drawn from the 90° corner to the hypotenuse. That altitude creates two smaller right triangles.

Why are the triangles similar?

Each small triangle has a 90° angle. Each also shares one acute angle with the original triangle. Two matching angles are enough for AA similarity. So the large triangle and both smaller triangles all have the same shape.

Label the hypotenuse pieces

Call the left piece p and the right piece q. The whole hypotenuse is c=p+q.

The altitude relationship

Similarity gives h²=pq. In words: the altitude is the geometric mean of the two hypotenuse pieces.

Worked numbers: p=4 and q=9

Then h²=4×9=36, so h=6.

But what if the SAT asks for area?

Do not stop at h=6. The whole hypotenuse is c=4+9=13. Use the hypotenuse as the base and h as the height: area = 1/2×13×6 = 39.

What if it asks for a leg?

The leg next to p satisfies leg²=c·p. The other leg satisfies other leg²=c·q. Example: if c=25 and p=9, that leg is √(25×9)=15.

What I want you to recognize instantly

Altitude from the right angle to the hypotenuse is not a random line. It is a huge signal: similarity + geometric means.

What to write first on test day: Write: c=p+q, h²=pq

Common wrong turn

Finding h correctly does not mean you are done. Re-read what quantity the question actually asks for.

Guided example

The altitude divides a hypotenuse into segments 9 and 16. What is the altitude?

Use h²=pq. So h²=9×16.


5-question transfer check

Do not rush into these. First ask yourself: “What pattern from the lesson is hiding here?”

Near transfer
1. The altitude to a right triangle's hypotenuse divides it into segments 4 and 9. Find the altitude.
Different presentation
2. The altitude divides the hypotenuse into 5 and 20. Find the area of the original triangle.
SAT-style trap
3. A right triangle has hypotenuse 25. One hypotenuse segment adjacent to a leg is 9. Find that leg.
Strategy choice
4. The altitude divides a hypotenuse into 8 and 18. What is the altitude?
Far transfer
5. A right triangle's altitude to its hypotenuse divides the hypotenuse into 9 and 16. Find the shorter leg.
